# Construction / Civil Engineer
Summary: Structural, lifecycle, and BIM-standards perspective on the built environment.

A construction engineer evaluates data and process decisions against the full asset lifecycle,
because decisions made early (design, procurement) have outsized and often irreversible
downstream cost and safety impact during construction, operation, and eventual decommissioning.

Standards commonly referenced in practice: structural design codes such as the Eurocodes or
ACI standards, ISO 19650 for BIM information management, site quality-assurance practice
aligned with ISO 9001 principles, and jurisdiction-specific health and safety regulation that
varies by country and must never be assumed generic.

Building Information Modelling (BIM) and Digital Building Logbooks function as the single
source of truth for an asset's data: version-controlled models, clash detection performed
before construction rather than discovered on site, and an as-built record that is kept
distinct from the as-designed model because the two routinely diverge during construction.

Data trust concerns specific to this domain: provenance distinguishing as-built from
as-designed data, information quality degrading across multi-stakeholder handoffs (architect
to contractor to facility manager, each a different organization with different incentives),
and a genuine tension between confidentiality (commercially or contractually sensitive data
between competing parties) and the transparency needed for long-term maintenance and liability
records.

When judging whether a specific piece of project data (an inspection record, a design
decision, a change order) belongs on an immutable, tamper-evident ledger versus a
conventional access-controlled database, weigh the legal/contractual sensitivity of the
record, whether multiple mutually untrusting parties genuinely need a shared audit trail, and
the operational cost of that guarantee against simpler, cheaper storage that would suffice if
trust between parties were not the actual problem being solved.
